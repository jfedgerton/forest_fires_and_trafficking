### IV setup, in full, for slide 4

**Endogenous regressor.** `percent_area_burned` — share of municipal area burned
in the quarter, from MCD64A1. Built as `presence_q` (max over the quarter's three
monthly grids, so a cell burning twice is not double-counted) divided by
municipal area.

**Instrument.** Vapour pressure deficit, the atmospheric drying power of air.
Tetens/FAO-56:

```
es(T) = 0.6108 · exp(17.27·T / (T + 237.3))      [kPa]
VPD   = max(0, es(T_air) − es(T_dewpoint))
```

Three operationalizations, all retained:

| Name | Definition |
|---|---|
| `vpd_mean_q` | quarterly mean of daily `es(t2m_mean) − es(d2m_mean)` |
| `vpd_max_q` | quarterly mean of daily `es(t2m_max) − es(d2m_mean)` — **preferred** |
| `vpd_excess3_q` | quarterly mean of daily `max(0, VPDmax − 3.0 kPa)` |

**Construction detail worth one sentence on the slide.** VPD is computed daily
and then averaged, never from quarterly-average temperature. `es(T)` is convex,
so averaging temperature first understates mean VPD by an amount scaling with
Var(T) — which varies systematically by biome and season, making it a
non-classical measurement error correlated with treatment.

**Estimating equation.**

```
Stage 1:  Fire_mt   = π·VPD_mt + X'_mt γ + α_m + δ_t + u_mt
Stage 2:  Y_mt      = β·Fire_hat_mt + X'_mt γ + α_m + δ_t + ε_mt
```

α_m municipality FE, δ_t year-quarter FE, SEs two-way clustered on municipality
and year-quarter. All four outcome forms are 2SLS — a count on the left-hand side
is fine for linear IV; the coefficient is a level effect.

**Exclusion restriction, and the evidence for it.** VPD affects forced labour
only through fire. The obvious violation is that heat independently changes
agricultural labour demand. Tested: regressed each of 12 candidate controls on
VPD with the same FE. **None of the seven agricultural measures is moved by VPD**,
and 5 of 108 tests clear p<.05 against a chance expectation of 5.4. Adding or
removing controls changes the estimate in the fourth decimal.

**Monotonicity / LATE.** β is a local effect for municipality-quarters whose fire
responds to VPD. That matters for the OLS–IV gap (item 5).

## 3. Intensity measures — RAN LAST NIGHT

**Conclusion: the intensity null is underpowered for TF and reasonably tight for
UE. It is not one finding, and the memo currently overstates it.**

`intensity` = OLS on `log(1+y)` conditional on `y > 0`, 2SLS, same FE.

| | TF | UE |
|---|---|---|
| Muni-quarters with y>0 | 2,102 (0.47%) | 12,261 (2.75%) |
| Municipalities involved | 970 | 3,247 |
| **Singletons dropped by muni FE** | **596 of 970 (61%)** | 1,136 of 3,247 (35%) |
| Estimation sample | **1,471** | 10,813 |
| Estimate (SE) | 0.0150 (0.0168) | 0.0002 (0.0046) |
| **Minimum detectable effect** (80% power) | **0.047 log pts ≈ 4.8%** | **0.013 log pts ≈ 1.3%** |
| First-stage F | 53.9 | 102.8 |

Treatment variation is *not* the problem — SD of fire in the positive subsample
is 6.13 versus 6.01 in the full panel. The problem is that the sample falls from
439,128 to 1,471 for TF, and 61% of the municipalities that ever record a rescue
contribute exactly one positive quarter and are dropped by the municipality fixed
effect.

**Decisions for the morning:**
- For UE the claim holds: we can rule out intensity effects above about 1.3%.
- For TF we cannot rule out anything below ~5%. Saying "fire does not change how
  many" is too strong for TF.
- **Option:** drop the municipality FE for the intensity model and use state FE,
  which recovers the 596 singleton municipalities. Different identification, but
  it turns an uninformative null into an informative one. Worth discussing with
  Kim.
- **Fix needed:** the Kim memo says the intensity margin is "never significant …
  I think this is a finding rather than a limitation." For TF that is not
  supportable. I flagged this to Jared; edit before the meeting.

## 4. Why the lag results are not significant — RAN LAST NIGHT

**Conclusion: wide confidence intervals, not precise nulls. In every single
specification the contemporaneous estimate sits inside the lag model's 95% CI.**

Contemporaneous + lags (LALL), `vpd_max_q`, 6 controls, 2002–2021:

| DV | Form | Contemp. β | Lag-model β | Lag-model 95% CI | SE inflation | Contemp. inside CI? | MDE ÷ contemp. β |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TF | Binary | 0.00062 | 0.00048 | [−0.00012, 0.00108] | 1.69× | **Yes** | 1.38 |
| TF | Count | 0.01607 | −0.00102 | [−0.02955, 0.02750] | 2.01× | **Yes** | 2.54 |
| TF | Log(1+y) | 0.00170 | 0.00094 | [−0.00073, 0.00262] | 1.69× | **Yes** | 1.41 |
| UE | Binary | 0.00246 | 0.00073 | [−0.00181, 0.00327] | 1.31× | **Yes** | 1.48 |
| UE | Count | 0.01093 | 0.01083 | [−0.00466, 0.02632] | 1.58× | **Yes** | 2.02 |
| UE | Log(1+y) | 0.00310 | 0.00130 | [−0.00203, 0.00462] | 1.36× | **Yes** | 1.54 |

The last column is the punchline: the minimum effect the lag model could detect
at 80% power is **1.4 to 2.5 times larger than the contemporaneous estimate.**
The lag specification could not have found an effect of the size we estimate even
if that effect were exactly true. "Loses significance when lags are added" is a
statement about power, not about the effect.

Cause is mechanical. Three serially correlated fire terms instrumented by three
correlated VPD terms; the first-stage F falls from ~102 to ~25 and the standard
error inflates 1.3–2.0×.

**The lag-only specs behave differently and that is diagnostic.** For L1 and L2
the contemporaneous estimate is **outside** the CI in all 12 cases, and L2 is
significantly negative. They are estimating a different thing, not a noisier
version of the same thing — which is what you'd expect given
`corr(fire_t, fire_t−2) = −0.22` within municipality after the FE. Keep excluding
them, and say why.

**Suggested framing for Kim:** "The effect is contemporaneous. Adding lags costs
us half the first stage and we lose the power to detect it, but the point
estimate does not move and the contemporaneous value is inside every lag-model
interval. We are not seeing a contradiction, we are seeing a weaker test."

## 5. Other issues to raise — open

Ordered by how much damage each does in review.

**a. The OLS–IV gap is the biggest unanswered question.** Naive is null in 126 of
126 binary specifications; IV is ~23× larger for TF and sign-flipped for UE.
Attenuation of that size implies a signal-to-noise ratio around 4% in MCD64A1.
Either the measurement error is far worse than the literature assumes, or the
LATE applies to a narrow VPD-responsive subpopulation with a much larger effect.
Pick one and defend it. **Concrete test:** compare against an independent burned
area product (GFED5 files are already in `data/`) — if the IV/OLS ratio shrinks
with a better-measured treatment, that supports measurement error.

**b. Enforcement selection in TF.** The outcome is where inspectors went.
Inspectors are dispatched on tips and prior cases. `n_operations` exists in the
panel and lets you condition on inspection, which is a partial answer. Not yet in
the main tables.

**c. Fixed effects may be too coarse, and this is untested.** Every model uses a
*national* year-quarter effect. Brazilian fire seasonality differs sharply by
biome. `uf^year-quarter` is the obvious check and it has never been run —
roughly 250 specifications, 15 minutes. This is the single cheapest thing that
could change the answer, and a referee will ask.

**d. UE has 21% unknown residence.** 15,019 victims with no municipality. Tested
at the national quarterly level and it does not track fire (all |r| < 0.13), but
that test cannot rule out municipality-level selection because the unknown
records carry no municipality by construction.

**e. The 2022 census discontinuity.** 91 municipalities have genuine population
level shifts between the 2021 estimate and the 2022 census. Population only
enters as a control, so low risk, but know it's there.

**f. Count models are the noisiest form.** 74.6% (TF) and 60.3% (UE) significant
against 95.2% / 85.7% for binary, because a raw count carries intensive-margin
variance that contributes nothing but noise. Consistent with item 3.
